# Special Intervention

**Special Intervention** refers to elite, high-risk tactical operations conducted by specialized units (often called Special Intervention Teams, SWAT, GIGN, GSG 9, RAID, or similar "special intervention" groups worldwide). These units handle life-threatening scenarios that exceed standard police capabilities, prioritizing the preservation of innocent lives, de-escalation when possible, and precise resolution when force is required.

Tactics emphasize **speed, surprise, violence of action (when needed), superior planning, intelligence, teamwork, and adaptability**. Operations are divided into phases: **containment/negotiation**, **deliberate/planned action**, or **emergency/immediate response**.

### Core Phases and Tactics

1. **Initial Response and Containment**
   - Establish **inner and outer perimeters** quickly to isolate the threat and prevent escape or reinforcement.
   - Set up a **command post** (outside the inner perimeter) for coordination.
   - Deploy **sniper/observer teams** (precision long rifle teams) for overwatch, intelligence gathering, and potential targeted neutralization.
   - Evacuate civilians/injured from the danger zone.
   - Initiate **crisis negotiation** (primary non-lethal tool) via trained negotiators to buy time, gather intel, and seek peaceful surrender.

2. **Intelligence Gathering and Reconnaissance**
   - Use drones/UAVs, tactical robots, pole cameras, under-door cameras, fiber optics, audio bugs, or thermal imaging for real-time layout, suspect/hostage positions, and threat assessment.
   - Conduct covert surveillance to map entry points, weak spots, and escape routes.
   - Analyze blueprints, witness statements, and suspect background for behavioral profiling.

3. **Planning and Rehearsal**
   - Develop multiple plans: **deliberate** (planned hostage rescue with rehearsals), **contingency** (backup if things change), and **emergency** (immediate action for imminent harm).
   - Rehearse extensively (dry runs, live-fire, force-on-force) to build muscle memory and coordination.
   - Prioritize safety model: hostages > officers > suspects.

4. **Dynamic / Deliberate Entry (Assault Phase)**
   - **Dynamic Entry**: High-speed surprise assault using **speed, surprise, and controlled violence** — ideal for active threats or imminent hostage harm.
     - Breach (mechanical, ballistic, explosive, thermal) doors/windows.
     - Deploy flashbangs/stun grenades, CS gas, or less-lethal munitions to disorient.
     - Enter in tight stacks/formations (e.g., buttonhook, cross-entry, flow techniques).
     - Clear rooms methodically: pie corners, threshold evaluation, strong wall positioning, triangulation for cross-coverage.
     - Neutralize threats rapidly while isolating/protecting hostages (often resolve at threshold/doorframe to minimize exposure).
   - **Deliberate Entry**: Slower, methodical clearing for complex structures or when precision outweighs speed.
     - Use stealth, diversion, or slow breach-and-hold.
     - Employ robots or tech for initial clearing.
   - Specialized techniques:
     - **Close Quarters Battle (CQB)**: Room entry, pieing corners, fatal funnel avoidance.
     - **Bounding/overwatch**: Teams advance under mutual cover.
     - **Hostage rescue specifics**: Prioritize hostage safety — use verbal commands, physical separation of hostage/threat, rapid extraction.

5. **Specialized Sub-Tactics**
   - **Sniper Operations**: Precision shots for neutralization or support (counter-sniper).
   - **Breaching**: Explosive, mechanical (rams, Halligan tools), ballistic, or hydraulic.
   - **Less-Lethal Options**: Beanbag rounds, tasers, chemical agents, or acoustic devices preferred over lethal force when feasible.
   - **Maritime/Aviation/Vertical**: Fast-roping, rappelling, boat insertions, or aircraft assaults (e.g., Lufthansa-style hijacking rescues).
   - **Counter-Ambush / Immediate Action Drills**: Rapid response to surprises.

6. **Resolution and Post-Operation**
   - Secure scene, provide immediate medical aid (often with embedded tactical medics/TEMS).
   - Extract hostages/suspects, conduct evidence preservation.
   - Debrief thoroughly to refine future tactics.

### Key Principles

- **Team Roles**: Team leader/commander, breachers, assaulters, snipers, medics, negotiators, robot/tech operators.
- **Training Emphasis**: Realistic scenarios, stress inoculation, inter-unit cooperation (e.g., GIGN/GSG 9/FBI HRT joint exercises).
- **Equipment Integration**: Ballistic shields, night vision, suppressors, armored vehicles for approach/cover.

These tactics evolve constantly based on real-world lessons (e.g., from Munich 1972 failures leading to GSG 9 creation, or modern active shooter/terrorism responses). Success relies more on **precise decision-making, communication, and adaptability** than sheer firepower. Units like GIGN, GSG 9, or top-tier SWAT (e.g., FBI HRT) are benchmarks for excellence in this domain due to rigorous selection, ongoing training, and proven track records in hostage rescues and counter-terrorism.
