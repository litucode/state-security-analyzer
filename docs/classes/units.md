# Units

## Description

Units are organized tactical formations of security personnel grouped by operational capability, specialization, and deployment context. Units form the backbone of security operations, coordinating individual agents into cohesive teams for enhanced effectiveness, protection, and mission accomplishment. Each unit type serves specific operational roles, from routine patrols to high-risk specialized interventions, and is composed of trained personnel with complementary equipment and expertise.

## Types

- **K9** — Canine units paired with trained officers; specialized for detection (explosives, narcotics, tracking), apprehension, area denial, and intimidation during tactical operations. Force multiplier for search, rescue, and threat identification.
- **Special Operations Forces (SOF)** — Elite multi-personnel tactical teams trained for high-risk operations, dynamic entries, counter-terrorism, hostage rescue, and specialized interventions requiring advanced tactics, weapons, and coordination.
- **Two-man Patrols (Binomes)** — Smallest mobile unit; two trained officers coordinating routine patrols, initial response, community engagement, and baseline threat assessment across assigned sectors.

## Specifications by Unit Type

### K9 Units

**Unit Composition:**

- **Personnel**: 1-2 officers (handler + backup/support officer)
- **Canine**: 1 trained dog (typically German Shepherd, Belgian Malinois, or Labrador Retriever)
- **Total Team Size**: 2-3 individuals + 1 animal

**Canine Specifications:**

- **Age Range**: 2-8 years (peak working years)
- **Physical Condition**: Excellent (regular fitness training)
- **Certifications**: Narcotics detection, explosives detection, tracking, apprehension, or area denial (specialized training)
- **Work Duration**: 4-6 hours per shift (rest periods required for animal welfare)
- **Rest Requirements**: Minimum 8-12 hours between shifts
- **Lifespan in Service**: 7-10 years (retirement upon decline in performance)

**Handler Equipment:**

- Duty belt with tools (handcuffs, radio, flashlight, baton, restraints)
- High-visibility vest (distinguishing K9 unit)
- Radio communication system (encrypted, multi-channel)
- Body camera (recording K9 deployment and actions)
- Leash and control harness (specialized, dual-clip for control)
- Muzzle (optional, situational deployment)
- First aid kit (human + veterinary trauma supplies)
- Treats and reward system (positive reinforcement tools)
- Medical monitoring equipment (heart rate monitor for canine fatigue detection)

**Canine Equipment:**

- Tactical harness (impact-resistant, equipment attachment ring)
- Protective vest (ballistic-resistant, dog-sized protection)
- Tracking collar with GPS (location monitoring)
- Detection equipment (scent article containers, odor samples)
- Booties/paw protection (for extreme terrain/weather)
- Water bowl and hydration pack (portable water system)

**Protective Features:**

- Canine armor/vest (optional, for high-threat operations)
- Handler body armor (Level II/IIIA, standard for armed units)
- Communication redundancy (backup radio channels)
- Veterinary medical support (rapid evacuation protocol for injured K9)
- Air-conditioned rest facility (animal welfare during warm weather)
- Mental enrichment protocol (stress management for canine welfare)

**Capabilities & Specializations:**

- **Narcotics Detection**: Identify controlled substances in baggage, vehicles, buildings; accuracy 95%+
- **Explosives Detection**: Locate explosive devices and components; trained to alert on specific odor profiles
- **Tracking**: Follow human scent trails over distances (up to 2+ km depending on conditions)
- **Apprehension**: Controlled bite and hold on suspect; handler commands for release
- **Area Denial**: Presence deterrence and active crowd management
- **Rescue**: Search for missing persons or survivors in collapsed structures

**Operational Notes:**

- K9 units deploy in high-threat or specialized search scenarios; reserve asset
- Canine welfare is priority; rest, hydration, temperature control monitored continuously
- Handler-canine bond critical; unit reliability depends on team cohesion and trust
- Retirement plan includes adoption or sanctuary placement after service

### Special Operations Forces (SOF)

**Unit Composition:**

- **Team Size**: 6-12 personnel (standard: 8 personnel)
- **Leadership**: 1 Team Leader (TL) + 1 Deputy Team Leader (DTL)
- **Operators**: 4-6 tactical operators with specialized roles
- **Support**: 1-2 support personnel (logistics, communications, medical)
- **Total Personnel**: 8-12 trained specialists

**Personnel Roles:**

- **Team Leader**: Overall unit command, tactical planning, real-time decision-making
- **Deputy Team Leader**: Second in command, assault element coordination
- **Breacher**: Dynamic entry specialist, explosive/non-explosive breach techniques
- **Marksman/Sniper**: Precision engagement, overwatch, threat neutralization
- **Medic**: Tactical emergency medical care, casualty management, CASEVAC coordination
- **Communications Specialist**: Encrypted comms, liaison with command, real-time intelligence relay
- **Operator (General)**: Core assault personnel, versatile in tactics and weapons handling

**Equipment per Operator:**

**Weapons:**

- Primary rifle (5.56mm NATO, 6.5mm Creedmoor, or equivalent; modular configurations)
- Secondary sidearm (9mm or .40 caliber; duty pistol)
- Backup blade/knife (tactical, multi-tool)
- Breaching tools (hammers, halligan bars, explosive charges)
- Less-lethal weapons (40mm launcher, CS gas canisters, rubber projectiles)
- Flashbang grenades or stun devices

**Tactical Gear:**

- Combat plate carrier (load-bearing vest, ballistic Level IV)
- Ballistic plates (front, back, side protection; ceramic or steel)
- Magazine pouches (10-15 loaded magazines per operator)
- Tactical helmet (ballistic Level IIIA+, night vision compatible)
- Tactical gloves (cut-resistant, dexterous fingertips)
- Combat boots (tactical, ankle support, slip-resistant)
- Knee and elbow pads (impact protection)
- Throat/neck protector (ballistic-resistant)

**Communications & Navigation:**

- Encrypted tactical radio (frequency-hopping, secure protocol)
- Helmet-mounted radio headset
- GPS/GNSS device with map display
- Laser range finder (targeting and distance measurement)
- Backup compass and map case

**Support Equipment:**

- Trauma/first aid kit (advanced items: tourniquets, hemostatic gauze, clotting agents)
- Rappelling equipment (carabiners, ropes, anchors for vertical deployment)
- Flashlights (LED, tactical, multiple color modes for night operations)
- Goggles (ballistic-resistant, interchangeable lenses)
- Gas mask or respirator (NBC protection)

**Team Level Equipment:**

- Tactical vehicle (armored SRU or transport)
- Breach equipment staging (portable tools, explosives box)
- Command post communication suite (mobile command center)
- Drone surveillance system (aerial reconnaissance capability)
- Thermal imaging equipment (FLIR for threat detection)
- Negotiation or observation post equipment (for barricaded suspect ops)

**Protective Features:**

- Full ballistic coverage (Level III/IV armor)
- Redundant communications (primary + backup channels)
- Medical support (on-team medic + rapid evacuation protocol)
- Overhead support (air cover via helicopter or drone)
- Command liaison (direct link to operational command)
- Rules of engagement (clear protocol to minimize civilian casualties)

**Operational Training:**

- Close Quarter Combat (CQB) proficiency
- Dynamic entry and breach techniques
- Hostage rescue and negotiation awareness
- Vehicle assault and extraction
- Fast-rope insertion from helicopter (HALO/HALO operations)
- Room entry, target identification, and selective engagement
- Casualty management and emergency procedures
- Counter-ambush and tactical evasion

**Deployment Scenarios:**

- High-risk warrant service (barricaded suspects, armed individuals)
- Hostage rescue operations
- Counter-terrorism response
- VIP protection during high-threat events
- Specialized tactical operations requiring elite capability
- Emergency response to active threats
- Reserve asset activated on escalation or specialized requirement

### Two-man Patrols (Binomes)

**Unit Composition:**

- **Personnel**: 2 trained officers
- **Rank**: Officer 1 (Senior/Lead) + Officer 2 (Support/Junior)
- **Total Team Size**: 2 individuals

**Officer Roles:**

- **Senior Officer**: Tactical lead, initial decision-making, area assessment, communication with dispatch
- **Support Officer**: Secondary observation, coverage during engagement, equipment management, backup decision authority

**Equipment per Officer:**

**Personal Equipment:**

- Duty belt (quick-access to tools)
- Handcuffs and restraints
- Radio/communication device (encrypted two-way portable radio)
- Flashlight (tactical, high-lumen, 500-1000 lm)
- Baton or impact weapon
- Pepper spray / OC spray (incapacitant)
- Taser / conducted energy weapon (where authorized)
- Firearm / sidearm (duty pistol) + extra magazines (2-3 mags minimum)
- Holster and magazine pouches
- Body camera (recording interactions and incidents)
- Notebook, pen, and ID/badge
- Basic first aid kit (bandages, antiseptic, trauma items)
- Multi-tool or utility knife

**Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):**

- Tactical helmet (ballistic-resistant, Level IIIA)
- Bullet-proof vest / body armor (Level II/IIIA)
- Tactical gloves (cut-resistant, padded)
- Ballistic eyewear / tactical goggles
- Knee & elbow pads (impact protection)
- Boots (tactical, steel-toed, slip-resistant)
- High-visibility vest (traffic control, active patrol deterrence)

**Team Level Equipment:**

- Patrol vehicle (standard or lightly armored SUV)
- Mobile data terminal (MDT) or tablet (dispatch, mapping, database access)
- GPS/GNSS navigation system
- Dash camera and rear-view recording system
- Emergency equipment (flares, cones, warning triangles)
- Portable barrier systems (small cordons)
- First aid and trauma kit (team-level supplies)
- Spotlight/searchlight (area illumination)
- Traffic control equipment (signs, cones, tape)
- Communication relay capability

**Protective Features:**

- Body armor for ballistic threats
- Vehicle protection (reinforced windows, doors)
- Communication redundancy (radio + cell phone backup)
- Dispatch monitoring and response coordination
- Located tracking (vehicle GPS visible to command)
- Rules of engagement (clear escalation protocol)

**Operational Capabilities:**

- **Patrol Operations**: Systematic area coverage, visible deterrence, threat scanning
- **Rapid Response**: Initial response to calls for service, incident assessment
- **Traffic Control**: Accident scene management, traffic direction, crowd control
- **Community Engagement**: Public interaction, information gathering, relationship building
- **Investigation**: Initial incident investigation, evidence preservation, witness statements
- **Assistance**: Welfare checks, lockout support, emergency assistance
- **De-escalation**: Conflict mitigation, verbal communication skills, tension reduction
- **Backup Support**: Secondary unit for higher-risk single-unit calls

**Deployment Context:**

- Primary unit for routine patrol operations
- Initial responders to low-to-medium risk calls
- Baseline security presence in assigned sectors
- Mobile deterrent for criminal activity
- Community policing and relationship building
- Supported by intervention or SOF teams for escalation
- Standard deployment across all operational shifts

**Shift Patterns:**

- 8-hour shifts (typical day/night patrol)
- 12-hour shifts (extended operations or critical periods)
- Rest cycles (48 hours rotation recommended for sustained operations)
- Integration with sector rotation (geographic area assignments)

## Unit Integration & Operational Hierarchy

**Deployment Strategy:**

1. **Normal Operations**: Binomes conduct routine patrols and initial response
2. **Escalation - Medium Threat**: Intervention teams deploy; Binomes provide perimeter support
3. **Escalation - High Threat**: SOF units deploy; Binomes secure outer perimeter
4. **Specialized Operations**: K9 units deploy for search/detection; other units provide security

**Communication Chain:**

- Binome officers → Sector Command
- Intervention Team Lead → Operational Command
- SOF Team Leader → Tactical Command
- K9 Handler → Specialized Operations Coordinator

**Resource Allocation:**

- K9 units: Reserve asset, scheduled deployment for specialized tasks
- SOF units: Reserve asset, escalation response for high-risk situations
- Binomes: Continuous deployment across sectors and shifts

**Casualty Management (Officer Down Protocol):**

- Binome casualty: SOF or Intervention team assists; evacuation via patrol vehicle or helicopter
- SOF casualty: CASEVAC via helicopter prioritized; support team remains operational
- K9 casualty: Handler and backup officer manage; veterinary support activated
